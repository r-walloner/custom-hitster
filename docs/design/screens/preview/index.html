Jamster — screens

The app's screens as built, on the design system, on a landscape phone (844 × 390). A board shows the variants that look different side by side (host and guest, the player on turn and the others). Smaller differences (a caption, one more button, another sentence) stay in the board's props, the canvas's Tweaks; in a preview, pass them as a query: RoomGone.html?reason=closed, AddPlayer.html?kind=rename, Reconnecting.html?state=failed. Boards with working controls are live: tap gaps on Placing and Challenger's guess, toggle Named the song on the reveal, pick sources on Your songs, step the settings, and pick a speaker.

Entry

Why it looks like this

  • Home is the first impression, so it borrows the cover art: the bobbing cassette from the design system with song cards floating around it.
  • Home is the one menu outside a room. Logged out: join with the four-letter code (no account), or log in with Spotify. Logged in: who you are with Account, the code, Host a game, and Your songs with its song count (or the import's bar).
  • The sign-in error keeps the Home art and only swaps the panel. It says what happened in plain words and offers the next step. One board covers every reason (see Tweaks).
  • Account is a pop-up over Home: your name (Change), Sign out, and Delete my account, which asks once more in its own pop-up.
  • Your songs picks what feeds the deck: liked songs, top songs, recent plays and playlists, with search and All / None. Picking a source imports it; its row shows a bar while it does. Refresh library and when it was imported sit in the header.
  • Portrait phones get one line asking to turn the phone sideways.
  • Only the logo, the tape and the pick cards tilt. The Home slogan is the one exception, cover only.

Joining a room

Why it looks like this

  • Joining asks for a name only. The people already at the table show beside it, so you know you're in the right room. Signed in, the field starts with the first word of your Spotify name.
  • Every screen around the game says where you are in the header: Mara's room, players, First to N.
  • A code that leads nowhere, or a room the host closed, gets the Home art with what happened and Back.
  • While there's nothing else to show (joining, signing in, the server gone), the logo and one line.
  • Action feedback is one toast at a time, bottom centre, gone after 4 seconds. Music that didn't start is a toast with Retry, not a screen of its own.
  • Toast wording: what happened, then one short fact. Nothing more.

Before the game

Why it looks like this

  • One lobby for everyone, in three columns: getting people in (code, QR, copy link), who's in, and setup. The host gets Close room and Start game; a guest gets Leave room and Waiting for Mara.
  • Each player renames their own seat (pencil); the host removes the others (✕). The Music badge marks whose Spotify plays.
  • Local players have no phone. The host adds them here and plays their turns on this device.
  • Music plays on, Your songs and Rules are matching rows with the same button: Change (View for a guest's rules, Sign in without Spotify). The details live on their own screens.
  • The speaker screen picks how the room hears the music (one speaker or everyone's own) and your device; the picked device has Test, which plays 10 seconds of one of your songs.
  • Settings are one line each: steppers and toggles, plain numbers, no coins. A guest sees the values.
  • Dealing shows each opening card face up as it lands, so the wait reads as work.
  • Closing the room takes a second tap. Middle dots only in the header; everywhere else commas and full stops.

During the game

One round, in order, then the menu and the pop-ups over it. Each phase is shown for the device that acts and for the ones that watch.

Why it looks like this

  • Landscape phone (844×390). Cards stay near-square, like real cards; the whole round fits one screen.
  • Timeline owns the full width at the bottom. Fixed 112px cards; tap a dashed + gap to place the pick. Too many cards: it scrolls sideways (the mouse wheel too), with a bar.
  • One centre panel holds the only decision right now, with the phase name top left and the countdown ring top right. Every button in it has the same height and lettering.
  • Placing: Lock it in, Skip song ●1 and Buy this card ●3 (the player on turn buys the card in play instead of guessing). An away player's turn can be skipped by the host, with a second tap.
  • Challenge: the first to challenge is the only challenger. One device, one decision: a single Challenge ●1 for your own player, a row each when the host decides for several local players.
  • The host plays only for local players, inside the UI (per-player rows). No "play as" dropdown.
  • Only the host reveals early and judges Named the song; the host and the player on turn may restart the song.
  • Tape always visible: reels and bounce show music is playing.
  • Compact scoreboard on every screen: initial, cards, tokens. Tap it for the full scoreboard.
  • Every pick is the same yellow card with a name; a locked pick turns tinted.
  • Reveal: the cover, title, artist and whose libraries it came from; the board's ✓ / ✕ marks say who was right. A card that went elsewhere shows dashed where it belongs.
  • Only the host moves on: Named the song ●+1, Keep listening, Next song. Everyone else sees "Mara named the song ●+1" once it's given.
  • One countdown style everywhere: the filling ring.

The menu and its pop-ups

  • The gear menu is a centred pop-up, like the others, with only what's needed mid-game: where the music plays (Change), Scoreboard, and End game (second tap) for the host or Leave game for a guest.
  • No "play as" option: the board already follows whose turn it is.
  • Scoreboard: best first, progress to the goal, tokens and each player's timeline in short form. On turn, Leading, Local player and That's you as notes; the player on turn is highlighted.
  • Reconnecting says what happened, then reassures: "Connection dropped. Your progress is safe." The game stays visible behind a dim layer.

After the game

Why it looks like this

  • It reuses the Home art, but the floating cards are the winner's own timeline. The card that won it sits in front, in the card-just-won style.
  • "Mara wins!" is the one big line in Rubik Mono One ("Mara and Sam win!" on a tie). One sentence below names the near misses.
  • The text has its own block, so a long name pushes the art down instead of covering it.
  • Final scoreboard on the right. The host gets Play again and Back to the lobby; everyone else Waiting for Mara and Leave room.
