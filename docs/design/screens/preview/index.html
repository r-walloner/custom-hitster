Jamster — screens

The app's screens, designed on the design system, on a landscape phone (844 × 390). Boards with working controls are live: tap gaps and card halves on Placing and Challengers guess, toggle Named it on the reveal, and pick playlists on the account screen.

Entry

Why it looks like this

  • Home is the first impression, so it borrows the cover art: the bobbing cassette from the design system with song cards floating around it.
  • One panel, two paths: join with the four-letter code (no account), or host with Spotify.
  • The sign-in error keeps the Home art and only swaps the panel. It says what happened in plain words and offers the next step. One board covers every reason.
  • Account: the library panel is for choosing what feeds the deck. Liked songs pinned on top, search, All / None, one checkbox per playlist or artist.
  • Re-import and Delete sit together under Your data, out of the way of picking.
  • Only the logo, the tape and the pick cards tilt. The Home slogan is the one exception, cover only.

Joining a room

Why it looks like this

  • Joining asks for a name only. The people already at the table show beside it, so you know you're in the right room.
  • Every screen around the game says where you are in the header: Mara's room, players, First to N.
  • Guests can add their library later from the lobby. It's optional and says how many Spotify spots are left.
  • Action feedback is one toast at a time, bottom centre, gone after 4 seconds. Playback problems are toasts too (music dropped → Retry, speaker failed → Change), not screens of their own.
  • Toast wording: what happened, then one short fact. Nothing more.

Before the game

Why it looks like this

  • Host lobby in three columns: getting people in (code, QR, copy link), who's in, and setup with Start game.
  • Local players have no phone. The host adds them here and plays their turns on this device.
  • Music and Rules are two matching rows with the same Change button. The details live on their own screens.
  • The speaker gets its own screen because it's the most common thing to go wrong: pick a device, play 5 seconds, confirm you heard it.
  • Settings are one line each: steppers and toggles, plain numbers, no coins.
  • Dealing shows each opening card as it lands, so the wait reads as work.
  • Middle dots only in the header; everywhere else commas and full stops.

During the game

One round, in order, then the menu and the pop-ups over it.

Why it looks like this

  • Landscape keeps cards near-square, like real cards; the whole round fits one screen.
  • The timeline owns the full width at the bottom. Fixed 112px cards with dashed + gaps; tap a gap or either half of a card. Too many cards: it scrolls sideways, with a bar.
  • One centre panel holds the only decision right now: Lock it in, Challenge / Pass, Lock guess, Next song. Big targets for tipsy thumbs.
  • The host plays for anyone inside the UI, on each player's own row. No "play as" dropdown.
  • The tape is always visible: reels and bounce show music is playing. Restart is a small icon beside the timer, since it's rarely used.
  • A compact scoreboard on every screen: initial, cards, tokens. Tapping it opens the full scoreboard.
  • Every pick is the same yellow card with a name. Locked picks turn tinted; the one guessing now stays bright, and so does their row.
  • Challengers guess in the order they challenged; guesses are visible, not secret.
  • Reveal: green ✓ and red ✕ marks with initials, same size and height. Library credit up front; the year shows only on the tape and the timeline.
  • One countdown style everywhere: the filling ring.
  • Named it is a single toggle. Buy a card stays (3 tokens, from the rules).
  • Wording is plain and short: "Challenge", "Guessing…", "Is that right?".
  • The gear menu is a centred pop-up, like the others, with only what's needed mid-game: where the music plays (Change), Scoreboard, End game.
  • Restart stays on the tape. Dropped playback is handled by a toast with Retry.
  • The scoreboard shows progress to the goal, tokens and each player's timeline in short form. The player on turn is highlighted.
  • Reconnecting says what happened, then reassures: "Connection dropped. Your progress is safe." The game stays visible behind a dim layer.

After the game

Why it looks like this

  • It reuses the Home art, but the floating cards are the winner's own timeline. The card that won it sits in front, in the card-just-won style.
  • "Mara wins!" is the one big line in Rubik Mono One. One sentence below names the near misses.
  • The text has its own block, so a long name pushes the art down instead of covering it.
  • Final scoreboard on the right; Play again and Back to the lobby at the bottom.

Not designed yet: a guest's in-game menu (leaving a running game), and portrait phones.
